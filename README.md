This entry explains a queue to a 10-year-old through a single analogy: a queue is a necklace made of people, where each person is a bead who can only shuffle forward when the bead in front moves. The first line lands that picture and quietly explains why a queue creeps forward one shuffle at a time and why only the person at the front gets served. The second line gives an honest account of the analogy — what it maps (the one-by-one shuffle, the single exit at the front) and what it leaves out (boredom, queue-jumping, giving up, and the joy when a second front opens).

## The entry

> A queue is a necklace made of people: you are one bead, and you can only shuffle forward when the bead in front shuffles too, all the way to the front, where one bead drops off at a time.
> The necklace maps the one-by-one shuffle and the single exit at the front, but leaves out the human parts: beads never get bored, push in, walk away, or cheer when a second front opens.
